Numerical laboratory

Original finite teaching experiments connecting the course’s models. Python computes exact rational values; these browser controls display binary floating-point approximations.

1. Learning: choose an action from expected loss

The stipulated loss matrix has clear/blocked rows and go/wait columns [[1,3],[11,3]]. Change the probability of blockage. The exact tie at20% chooses wait. These are assumed losses, not measured traffic risks.

Before changing the input: at 20% blockage, going has expected loss 0.8 × 1 + 0.2 × 11 = 3, equal to waiting. At 19%, going has loss 2.9. The probability axis is summed out before choosing an action; the two action losses are not themselves probabilities.

2. Planning: retries, discount and arrival

Compare three one-minute walking actions with repeated two-minute retry actions. A retry succeeds and ends the episode, or fails and returns to the same state. Its failure probability is p, the failure control divided by 100. Each action receives a reward equal to minus its duration in minutes. The discount factor γ is the discount control divided by 100 and applies once per decision transition, regardless of that action's duration. It is not a discount per minute. With γ = 1, the return is minus expected elapsed time when arrival has finite expected time. Try 100% failure with γ = 1, then reduce γ: a finite discounted return does not create an arrival.

At 40% failure and γ = 1, expected retry time solves T = 2 + 0.4T, so T = 10/3 minutes; walking takes 3. Under the declared discount per decision, retry return instead solves V = −2 + γpV, giving V = −2/(1 − γp) when γp < 1. Three walking actions give −(1 + γ + γ²). At 100% failure, retry never arrives: its undiscounted return diverges, while γ < 1 makes the continuing discounted sum finite. Compare the two actions under the same declared criterion. A discounted score is not an elapsed time.

3. Games: a mixture is not a revealed action

A maximizes and B minimizes the one-shot payoff [[2,-3],[-3,4]]. Each independently draws its action without revealing the realization. The controls give the first-action probability in twelfths. At7/12 each, security bounds coincide at−1/12. Revealing A’s sampled action lets B force−3; that is a different information structure.

If A’s first-action probability is p, B’s first pure action gives A payoff5p−3 and B’s second gives4−7p. Equalizing them yields p=7/12 and payoff−1/12. Maximizing their lower envelope establishes A’s security strategy here. This interior equalizer is not a universal recipe for every game.

4. Inference: normalize the stated evidence

A binary cause changes the probability of a positive observation. Sensitivity means P(positive|cause), and false positive means P(positive|no cause). Set both to zero to see why a zero-probability observation has no defined conditional posterior. This is an assumed probability table, not medical advice.

At the default values, cause-and-positive mass is0.1×0.9=0.09 and no-cause-and-positive mass is0.9×0.1=0.09. Normalizing gives posterior0.09/0.18=1/2. Sensitivity90% does not imply a90% posterior: the prior and alternative explanation also matter. This conditioning calculation does not identify a causal intervention.

5. Logic: truth in one model is not entailment

Evaluate (P or Q) and not(P and Q) under your chosen truth assignment. The knowledge base has two satisfying assignments. It entails P or Q, but does not entail P by itself. An observed true P in one model cannot establish truth in every model.

The satisfying assignments are(P=false,Q=true) and(P=true,Q=false). Both make P or Q true, while one makes P false. Entailment asks about every satisfying assignment; evaluation asks about the single assignment you entered. This finite enumeration is not a proof that an unrestricted first-order solver terminates.

6. Evaluation: aggregate loss and growth units

Model A has subgroup losses1%/80%; B has10%/20%. Change the first group’s share. A better average can coexist with a worse worst-group loss. The separate growth control stipulates a total ten-year change in basis points; mistakenly applying it every year compounds a different quantity. Neither input is an observed fairness result or economic forecast.

At 99% weight on the first group, A's average is 0.99 × 0.01 + 0.01 × 0.8 = 0.0179; B's is 0.101. Yet A's worst subgroup loss is 0.8, compared with B's 0.2. Separately, 53 basis points means a 0.0053 total proportional change. A ten-year level factor of 1.0053 differs from compounding 1.0053 ten times.

7. Research judgment: information has a cost

There are two equally likely worlds. A safe action pays2; a risky action pays0 or3. Perfect information lets you keep either action and has value1/2 before experiment cost. Change cost in tenths. At the exact tie, decline the experiment. This stipulated decision is not a universal rule for choosing a human career.

Without information, risky expected payoff is(0+3)/2=1.5, so choose safe payoff2. With perfect information, choose safe in the low world and risky in the high world: expected payoff(2+3)/2=2.5. Information is worth0.5 before cost. A0.4-cost experiment yields2.1; a0.6-cost experiment yields1.9 and should be declined under these stipulated preferences.
